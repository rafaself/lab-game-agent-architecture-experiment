# Combat and Encounter Design

Use this skill when designing or reviewing combat behavior and encounters. For code changes, follow the project's selected architecture and implementation conventions.

- Start from the player's documented movement, attacks, defensive options, and intended learning goals.
- Give each enemy or boss pattern a distinct combat role, readable cue, and player response. Avoid enemies that differ only in name.
- Make dangerous attacks understandable and avoidable using the game's actual controls and movement.
- Build room encounters that teach or combine established mechanics. Increase pressure without removing counterplay.
- Make elite and boss encounters distinct through behavior or attack patterns; use stat increases as supporting changes, not the only source of identity.
- Treat unprovided timings, damage values, and difficulty curves as tuning decisions. Label proposals as assumptions and don't claim balance without play evidence.

When a design artifact is useful, summarize each role or pattern with its purpose, cue, player response, and tuning assumptions. Stay within the supplied specification.
